# Module 3 — AI Research & Scriptwriting

[← Module 2](02-content-strategy.md) | [Course Home](../README.md) | [Next: Module 4 →](04-ai-video-generation.md)

## Goal

Learn how to use AI to speed up research and script creation without turning your channel into generic, repetitive content.

## 1. Separate Research From Writing

Do not ask an AI model to "research and write the final script" in one vague prompt.

Use a staged workflow:

```text
TOPIC
  ↓
QUESTIONS
  ↓
RESEARCH NOTES
  ↓
ANGLE
  ↓
OUTLINE
  ↓
HOOKS
  ↓
DRAFT
  ↓
FACT CHECK
  ↓
FINAL SCRIPT
```

This gives you more control and makes weak information easier to catch.

## 2. Start With a Research Brief

Before drafting, define exactly what you need to know.

Template:

```text
TOPIC:
TARGET VIEWER:
WHAT THEY ALREADY KNOW:
MAIN QUESTION:
SUB-QUESTIONS:
IMPORTANT FACTS TO VERIFY:
EXAMPLES NEEDED:
DESIRED TAKEAWAY:
```

## 3. Build a Useful Angle

The topic is not the angle.

Topic:

> AI video editing

Possible angles:

- AI video editing for complete beginners
- What AI editors still get wrong
- How to turn one podcast into 10 short clips
- AI editing vs manual editing
- My beginner workflow for making Shorts faster

The angle determines what belongs in the script and what should be removed.

## 4. Generate Hooks Separately

Your first sentence has a different job from the rest of the script: it must earn attention.

Ask AI for multiple hook styles rather than accepting the first output.

Useful categories:

- Curiosity
- Contrarian
- Problem-first
- Outcome-first
- Story
- Demonstration
- Mistake
- Comparison

Example prompt:

```text
Give me 15 opening hooks for a short video about [TOPIC].
The target viewer is [AUDIENCE].
The main promise is [PROMISE].
Keep each hook under 15 words.
Avoid fake urgency, clickbait that the video cannot satisfy, and generic phrases.
Group the hooks by style.
```

## 5. Outline Before Drafting

A simple short-form outline:

```text
HOOK
CONTEXT
VALUE / EXPLANATION
PROOF OR EXAMPLE
PAYOFF
CTA
```

A simple long-form outline:

```text
OPENING PROMISE
WHY THIS MATTERS
SECTION 1
SECTION 2
SECTION 3
EXAMPLE / DEMONSTRATION
COMMON MISTAKES
SUMMARY
NEXT ACTION
```

## 6. Write for Speech, Not Essays

Spoken scripts should sound natural when read aloud.

Prefer:

- Short sentences
- Concrete words
- One idea at a time
- Natural transitions
- Specific examples
- Occasional sentence fragments when they sound conversational

Avoid:

- Long introductions
- Empty motivational filler
- Repeating the same point three ways
- Corporate language
- Unnecessary definitions
- Overloaded sentences

## 7. Use AI as an Editor

One of the best uses of AI is improving a script you already shaped.

Useful editing prompts:

### Remove filler

```text
Tighten this script by 20% without removing useful information.
Delete repeated ideas, filler, throat-clearing, and unnecessary transitions.
Keep the original voice.
```

### Improve retention

```text
Review this script for audience retention.
Identify any section where the viewer is likely to lose interest.
For each weak section, explain why and rewrite only that section.
Do not add fake suspense.
```

### Make it more visual

```text
Mark every sentence that could be supported by B-roll, screen recording, text animation, chart, screenshot, or demonstration.
Suggest one visual for each marked sentence.
```

## 8. Fact Checking Is a Separate Step

AI can produce confident errors.

Before publishing factual content:

1. Highlight every claim that could be checked.
2. Verify important claims against reliable sources.
3. Check dates, prices, statistics, names, and product features.
4. Remove claims you cannot support.
5. Keep source links in your production notes when appropriate.

Do not treat generated citations as proof until you verify the underlying source.

## 9. Write for Repurposing

If a long-form video will later become Shorts, create sections that can survive outside the original context.

Instead of:

> "And the third thing I mentioned earlier is important here..."

Prefer:

> "The third mistake is automating before you understand the workflow."

The second version works much better as a standalone clip.

## 10. Build a Reusable Master Prompt

Create one prompt that reflects your channel instead of starting from zero each time.

Template:

```text
You are helping me create a video for my channel.

AUDIENCE:
[describe viewer]

CHANNEL PROMISE:
[what the viewer gets from the channel]

CONTENT PILLARS:
[list pillars]

VIDEO TOPIC:
[topic]

ANGLE:
[angle]

GOAL:
[what the viewer should understand, feel, or do]

STYLE:
- conversational
- concise
- specific
- no fake urgency
- no generic motivational filler
- examples whenever useful

DELIVER:
1. 10 hook options
2. Recommended hook with reasoning
3. Video outline
4. Full spoken script
5. Suggested visuals/B-roll
6. 5 title ideas
7. 3 short-form clip opportunities
8. Claims that should be fact-checked
```

Save this and improve it as your channel develops.
